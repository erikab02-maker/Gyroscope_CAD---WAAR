I designed a gyroscope with Skye Kaser for the Washington Aerial Robotics Club (WAAR) at the University of Washington during the 2025-2026 academic year. Designing the gyroscope involved multiple design iterations, where I learned from each design to improve the following iteration in such a way as to reduce materials, improve safety factors and stability, and meet design requirements. This entire process allowed me to go through the design cycle from brainstorming ideas, to parameterizing the object in SolidWorks, 3D printing components, assembling components in real life, testing the gyroscope and re-designing. I went through two iterations of the design, with the first iteration having some problems with tolerances, the functionality of the rotation of the base, and the use of excess materials. After finding the areas of improvement I collaborated with Skye on how we could re-design or alter pre-existing CAD to engineer a second iteration of the gyroscope. The final product had significant improvement with the connection to the ball bearings in the top octagon by re-designing the connector, the base rotated much more easily, the base stand was designed to have more support and prevent bending while being transported and overall, less material was used which prevented warping when printing the components. Through the process of working with another engineer to design the gyroscope, I learned collaboration and teamwork skills by communicating design ideas and concerns for the functionality of certain components. I also gained experience utilizing SolidWorks to create the individual components, and in engineering design by going through the entire cycle of designing, evaluating and re-designing.
